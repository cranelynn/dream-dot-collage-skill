# Dream Dot Collage

## Purpose
Create a polished, airy dream-core collage that feels gentle, surreal, and art-directed rather than cluttered. The signature composition combines a simple line-art sleeping puppy, one large solid-color circular dream space, and a few small image-derived cutout motifs.

## Non-negotiable rules
1. **One reference image = one independent result.** Use only the currently selected reference image as the source of extracted visual material. Never combine people, scenery, objects, or styles from separate reference images.
2. **Keep the dream circle simple.** The large circle contains a flat, solid color or a very subtle single-color treatment. Do not fill it with a photograph, scenic landscape, room, or complex illustrated environment.
3. **Extract, do not replace.** Select a few recognizable people, objects, silhouettes, or details from the reference photo. Present them as separate small cutout accents around or partly overlapping the circle. Preserve their identity and recognizable visual features.
4. **Keep the puppy minimal.** Use a small, calm sleeping puppy drawn with a few confident, delicate lines. It is a quiet anchor, not a detailed mascot illustration.
5. **Design with restraint.** Establish one clear focal point, generous negative space, a limited palette, and a deliberate visual hierarchy. Every decorative mark must have a reason to exist.
6. **Do not imitate a specific artist.** Use the general visual principles described here rather than copying a named artist's signature style or an existing artwork.

## Workflow
1. Inspect the single supplied image and identify its main subject, distinctive secondary objects, dominant colors, and emotional tone.
2. Select only 1–3 useful source elements. Prefer instantly recognizable shapes over many tiny fragments.
3. Choose a restrained background and one contrasting solid color for the dream circle. Coordinate colors with the source image without forcing every original color into the palette.
4. Compose the large circle first, then place the sleeping puppy and extracted cutouts with intentional spacing.
5. Add at most a few simple dream symbols, such as a candy, an origami crane, a tiny star, or a curved orbit line. They should support the composition, not compete with the source elements.
6. Check source fidelity, readability at thumbnail size, spacing, and visual restraint before finalizing.

## Composition defaults
- One large circle as the dominant shape.
- One small line-art sleeping puppy, preferably near the circle's edge or below it.
- 1–3 isolated photo-derived cutouts from the same reference image.
- 1–3 simple symbolic accents at most.
- A quiet background, clear margins, and deliberate asymmetry.
- A cohesive palette: usually 2–4 main colors plus natural source-image colors.

## Output expectations
Produce one finished image, not a mood board, multi-panel sheet, or collection of alternatives unless explicitly requested. Preserve important identity cues in extracted people and objects. Keep the result editorial, soft, and dreamlike, with a clear focal hierarchy.

## Avoid
Photographic scenes inside the circle, multiple reference images fused together, sticker-sheet layouts, random floating objects, excessive stars and sparkles, heavy glow, thick cartoon outlines, noisy textures, forced purple palettes, over-rendered puppies, distorted faces, and source elements that become unrecognizable.
