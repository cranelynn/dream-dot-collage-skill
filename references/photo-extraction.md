# Photo Extraction Rules

## Source boundary
Only extract visual content from the one reference image currently being processed. Never borrow people, locations, objects, or details from another uploaded image. When multiple images are supplied without clear selection, process them separately or ask which one should be used.

## What to extract
Prioritize elements that carry personal meaning or are visually distinctive:
- A recognizable person or pet.
- A memorable object.
- A clear silhouette, accessory, flower, building detail, or travel-related object.
- A small detail whose shape and color remain legible when reduced.

Select 1–3 elements by default. Do not extract every object in the image.

## Fidelity
Preserve identity-defining features, face shape, hairstyle, clothing cues, object proportions, and distinctive colors where visible. Do not invent new facial details or alter the subject's identity. If an element is too small, obscured, or low-resolution to extract faithfully, omit it rather than hallucinating detail.

## Cutout treatment
- Separate each selected element from its original background.
- Keep the cutout edge clean and natural; avoid thick white sticker borders unless explicitly requested.
- Preserve enough photographic detail to make the subject recognizable.
- A subtle shadow or thin outline may be used only when needed for separation.
- Do not flatten every photo element into generic clip art.

## Placement
Treat cutouts as independent accents, not as a replacement for the circle's solid-color interior. They may overlap the circle boundary slightly or float nearby with clear spacing. Keep faces and other important details unobstructed.

## Integrity
Do not merge people or objects from different images. Do not reconstruct unseen portions of a person or object beyond what the reference supports. Avoid stretching, liquifying, duplicating, or materially changing extracted subjects.
